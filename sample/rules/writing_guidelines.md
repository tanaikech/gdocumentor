# Academic & Technical Writing Guidelines

## 1. Eliminate Redundancies, Tautologies & Double Comparatives/Superlatives
- Never use double comparatives or double superlatives (e.g., avoid *"most biggest"*, *"more better"*).
- Eliminate redundant word pairs and tautologies (e.g., avoid *"many various"*, *"connect and integrate"*, *"every single day"*, *"completely eliminate"*).

## 2. Correct Usage of Uncountable Nouns
- Treat mass/uncountable nouns such as **software**, **work**, **information**, **equipment**, and **research** as singular uncountable nouns.
- Never pluralize them directly (e.g., write *"specialized software"* instead of *"special softwares"*, and *"daily routine tasks"* or *"routine work"* instead of *"routine works"*).

## 3. Avoid Colloquial Fillers & Weak Intensifiers
- Remove conversational filler adverbs and vague intensifiers in technical or academic prose, including *"basically"*, *"super"*, *"totally"*, *"at all"*, and *"very"*.
- Replace awkward collocations like *"is very recommended"* with precise academic phrasing (e.g., *"is highly recommended"* or *"serves as an effective starting point"*).

## 4. Prefer Active Voice & Direct Clause Construction
- Avoid empty expletive or nominalized openings such as *"There is a merit that..."* or *"The advantage of X is because..."* (use *"A key advantage of X is that..."* or state the subject and action directly).
- Replace wordy passive chains (e.g., *"is a coding platform that is provided by Google, and it is made based on..."*) with concise active phrasing (e.g., *"is a Google-provided scripting platform built on JavaScript"*).

## 5. Integrate Figures & Tables into the Main Narrative
- Explicitly reference embedded tables and figures within the body paragraphs (e.g., *"As summarized in Table 1..."* or *"Figure 1 illustrates..."*) and ensure all visual labels and units are self-explanatory.
